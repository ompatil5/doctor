Diabetes Care in Mumbai, With Online Consultation Across India

Personalised, unhurried care for type 1, type 2, prediabetes and gestational diabetes.

Diabetes is not one disease and it is not one treatment. A thirty-year-old with type 1, a fifty-five-year-old with long-standing type 2, and a young woman with gestational diabetes need three very different conversations. My job is to work out which conversation you need.

Type 2 diabetes

Most people I see have type 2 diabetes, and many have been told the same three things: take the tablet, cut sugar, walk more. Those are fine, but not a plan. At your first visit I look at your reports, your daily routine and your medicines, and we decide together what to change first. That may mean adjusting or simplifying medication, bringing in newer drug classes where they are appropriate, or looking at sleep and stress.

Some people, especially those diagnosed early, can bring their sugars into a non-diabetic range with sustained lifestyle change and sometimes medication support. I have written a book on this, Path to Diabetes Mellitus Type 2 Remission. I am careful with the word “reversal”, because remission needs follow-up and is not a cure. I will tell you honestly whether it is realistic for you.

Type 1 diabetes

Living with type 1 is a daily job, and technology can lighten it. I work with insulin regimens, insulin pumps, flash and continuous glucose monitoring, and carbohydrate counting. I have co-authored abstracts on insulin pump therapy and flash glucose monitoring presented at international meetings, and I am a member of ISPAD. I also have a long-term interest in practical, affordable care for people with type 1, which is why I work with T1International.

Prediabetes

Prediabetes is the best moment to act, and most people are told nothing useful about it. We look at your glucose, HbA1c, weight, liver and cholesterol picture together. I presented work on lifestyle intervention for reversing prediabetes, and my approach is simple: small changes you can keep.

Gestational diabetes and pregnancy

High sugar during pregnancy is frightening and often poorly explained. I support women through diagnosis, nutrition planning, glucose monitoring and, when needed, insulin, and I stay in touch after delivery, because the risk of later diabetes does not end with the baby.

Diabetic foot care

Foot problems are among the most preventable complications. I wrote Footprints of Wellness: Managing Foot Care in Diabetes, and I examine feet at every annual review.

Diabetes and surgery

If you have a planned operation, your sugar control before and after it matters. Perioperative diabetes management has been part of my work since my training at the Asian Heart Institute.

Diabetes distress and mental health

If you are tired of diabetes, that is a medical issue and not a weakness. I screen for it and talk about it openly.

When to see a doctor

Schedule a clinical review if your fasting glucose remains consistently above target, your HbA1c has changed unexpectedly, or you experience recurrent episodes of low blood sugar (shakiness, sweating, confusion). If you experience severe symptoms such as difficulty breathing, persistent vomiting, or unresponsiveness, please go immediately to the nearest hospital emergency room.
