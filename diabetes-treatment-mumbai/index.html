Types of diabetes

Type 1Autoimmune

Type 1 diabetes develops when the body can no longer make enough insulin. It needs ongoing insulin replacement and regular glucose monitoring.

Type 2Insulin resistance

Type 2 diabetes involves insulin resistance and a gradual change in insulin production. Care considers glucose, daily routine and related health risks together.

PrediabetesHigher risk

Prediabetes means blood glucose is higher than usual but not in the diabetes range. It is a useful time to review habits and overall metabolic health.

GestationalPregnancy

Gestational diabetes is first recognised during pregnancy. It needs coordinated monitoring during pregnancy and follow-up after delivery.

LADAAdult onset

LADA is an autoimmune form of diabetes that begins in adulthood and can initially resemble type 2 diabetes. Assessment helps clarify the diabetes type over time.

MODYInherited

MODY is a group of uncommon inherited forms of diabetes. A family pattern and age at diagnosis can guide further clinical assessment.

Stress-inducedDuring illness

Acute illness, injury or major physical stress can raise blood glucose. Results are interpreted in the context of recovery and previous glucose history.

Drug-inducedMedication-related

Some medicines can raise blood glucose or reveal an underlying tendency to diabetes. A clinician can review timing, other health conditions and monitoring needs.

Diabetes and surgery

For planned surgical procedures, glycaemic control before and after surgery is vital. Perioperative diabetes management has formed an integral part of Dr. Shaikh's clinical focus since early residency at the Asian Heart Institute.

Complications

Foot careDaily checks

Diabetes can reduce sensation or affect circulation in the feet. Regular foot checks and prompt attention to wounds can help prevent more serious problems.

EyeRetinopathy

Diabetes can affect the small blood vessels at the back of the eye. Eye screening can detect changes before they noticeably affect vision.

KidneyRegular checks

Diabetes can affect kidney filtering over time. Kidney blood tests and urine protein checks help identify changes early.

NervesNeuropathy

Nerve changes may cause tingling, burning, numbness or altered sensation, often in the feet. New or changing symptoms deserve clinical review.

EmotionalDiabetes distress

The daily work of diabetes can feel overwhelming. Diabetes distress is recognised and can be discussed as part of care; support is available when needed.

When to see a doctor

Schedule a clinical review if your fasting glucose remains consistently above target, your HbA1c has changed unexpectedly, or you experience recurrent episodes of low blood sugar (shakiness, sweating, confusion). If you experience severe symptoms such as difficulty breathing, persistent vomiting, or unresponsiveness, please go immediately to the nearest hospital emergency room.
