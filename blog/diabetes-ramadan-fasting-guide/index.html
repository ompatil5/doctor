Diabetes during Ramadan: a practical guide

By Dr. Firdous Shaikh • 5 min read • Medically reviewed

Key points

  • Many people with diabetes want to fast, and some can do so safely with preparation.
  • See your diabetes doctor four to eight weeks before Ramadan to review your risk and medicines.
  • Break the fast if your glucose is below 70 mg/dL (3.9 mmol/L) or above 300 mg/dL (16.7 mmol/L), or if you feel unwell [8].

Start with a conversation, not a decision

Islam exempts people who are ill, and that exemption can apply to diabetes. Many people still choose to fast, and my role is not to forbid you but to help you make an informed decision. Please speak with your doctor about your health and your religious scholar about the guidance, ideally before Ramadan begins.

Risk level

The IDF-DAR guidelines sort people into risk groups [8]. Those at very high risk, who are generally advised not to fast, include people with recent severe hypoglycaemia or hypoglycaemia unawareness, recent diabetic ketoacidosis, unstable or poorly controlled type 1 diabetes, advanced kidney disease or dialysis, pregnancy with diabetes, and acute illness. Your doctor will go through the full list with you.

Before Ramadan

  • Check your HbA1c, kidney function and blood pressure.
  • Review all medicines. Some, such as sulfonylureas and insulin, can cause dangerous lows during fasting and may need changing, so do not adjust them yourself.
  • Learn the symptoms of low and high sugar.
  • Agree a monitoring plan. Checking your sugar during the fast does not break it, according to the majority of scholars. Please confirm with your own scholar.

Suhoor and iftar

  • Eat suhoor as late as possible. Include protein, fibre and slow-release carbohydrates: eggs, dals, curd, oats, whole grain roti, vegetables.
  • Break the fast with water and a date or two, then a balanced meal. Avoid a very large iftar of fried foods and sweets, which pushes sugar up and makes the next day harder.
  • Drink plenty of water between iftar and suhoor.
  • Keep sweet drinks and dessert as a small treat and not the main course.

During the day

Avoid heavy exertion in the heat. Take any prayer-related activity at a gentle pace. Stop and check your glucose if you feel shaky, sweaty, confused or unusually thirsty.

When to break the fast

  • Glucose below 70 mg/dL (3.9 mmol/L) or falling rapidly
  • Glucose above 300 mg/dL (16.7 mmol/L)
  • Symptoms of low sugar, dehydration or illness

Breaking a fast for health is permitted. Your health is a trust you hold.

After Ramadan

See your doctor within two weeks to return to your usual medicines and review the month's readings.

Medically reviewed by Dr. Firdous Shaikh.

References

  1. Hassanein M, Afandi B, Yakoob Ahmedani M, et al. Diabetes and Ramadan: practical guidelines 2021 (IDF-DAR). Diabetes Res Clin Pract. 2022;185:109185.

Pre-Ramadan medication assessment

Schedule a clinical review four to eight weeks prior to fasting to adjust insulin or oral regimens safely and calculate your IDF-DAR risk score.
