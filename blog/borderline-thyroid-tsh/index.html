Thyroid Health & TSH

Why is my thyroid still "borderline" on every report?

By Dr. Firdous Shaikh • 4 min read • Medically reviewed

Key points

  • A “borderline” result usually means a mildly raised TSH with a normal T4, called subclinical hypothyroidism.
  • Whether to treat depends on how high the TSH is, your antibodies, your age, symptoms, and pregnancy plans.
  • A single reading is not enough. Repeat the test before deciding.

What is happening

Your pituitary gland releases TSH to tell the thyroid to make hormone. When the thyroid is a little sluggish, the pituitary shouts louder, and TSH rises first, while T4 still looks normal. That pattern is called subclinical hypothyroidism.

Why one report is not enough

TSH naturally varies through the day, rises after illness, and can be affected by biotin supplements, recent iodine exposure, and some medicines. Many mildly raised TSH values return to normal when repeated after six to twelve weeks. Treating on a single result can mean years of an unnecessary tablet.

When I lean toward treating

  • TSH is persistently high, particularly above 10 mIU/L [6]
  • You have positive thyroid antibodies (anti-TPO), which predict progression
  • You are pregnant or planning pregnancy, where thyroid control matters for both mother and baby
  • You have symptoms plus a repeatedly raised TSH, or a goitre
  • Cholesterol is high and unexplained

When I lean toward watching

For an otherwise well adult with a mildly raised TSH, normal antibodies and few symptoms, repeating the test in a few months is often reasonable. Older adults can have slightly higher normal TSH ranges, and treating them aggressively may do more harm than good.

“But I feel tired and my hair is falling”

I take that seriously, and I look beyond the thyroid. Iron deficiency, vitamin D and B12 levels, sleep, stress, diabetes, anaemia and depression can cause the same complaints. Thyroid is often blamed first and sometimes unfairly.

If you are on thyroxine

Take it on an empty stomach, with water, 30 to 60 minutes before breakfast, and keep calcium and iron supplements at least four hours away from it. Get your TSH rechecked about six to eight weeks after any dose change.

References

  1. Jonklaas J, Bianco AC, Bauer AJ, et al. Guidelines for the treatment of hypothyroidism (American Thyroid Association). Thyroid. 2014;24(12):1670-1751. Also check the more recent ATA and Indian Thyroid Society guidance for your own reference.

Evaluate your thyroid reports

If you have persistent fatigue or recurring borderline thyroid test results, book an in-person or video consultation for an objective assessment.
