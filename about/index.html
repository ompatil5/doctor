Biographical Background

About Dr. Firdous Shaikh

I trained as a doctor at Zhejiang University School of Medicine in China, finished my internship there in 2012, and passed the Foreign Medical Graduate Examination in India on my first attempt. My early years back home were in emergency medicine as a Chief Medical Officer at Lady Hardinge Medical College in Delhi. Emergency work teaches you to decide quickly and communicate clearly, and I still lean on both.

Diabetes pulled me in because it is a condition where a doctor can change a life slowly and visibly. I completed my Diabetology training at the Asian Heart Institute in Mumbai, working in the Department of Diabetology and Endocrinology, where I handled perioperative diabetes care, meaning keeping blood sugar safe around surgery. I went on to earn a postgraduate qualification in Clinical Endocrinology and Diabetes from the Royal College of Physicians, UK.

From 2020 to 2022 I worked as a Consultant Diabetologist at the BKC dedicated COVID Health Centre in Mumbai. We were managing more than 500 patients with diabetes at once, so I built a real-time glucose charting system on Google Sheets that let the whole team see who needed attention first. It was not elegant technology, but it worked, and I was recognised for that service by the city.

Since then I have worked in clinics and community settings across Mumbai, run diabetes detection camps for school children, police personnel and underserved communities, and continued to study. I completed a Certificate Course in Clinical Diabetology and Obesity at Harvard Medical School and an advanced diabetes course at Emory School of Medicine in 2024.

Alongside patients, I do research and write. My work has been presented at ATTD, the American Diabetes Association Scientific Sessions and national conferences in India, and I have co-authored a published study on diabetes distress in Indian patients. I have also written two books for patients and colleagues.

What I care about most

  • The emotional side of diabetes. Diabetes distress is real and under-treated. I am a co-author of work on developing a Modified Diabetes Distress Scale for India, and I serve on the Diabetes India Mental Health Group.
  • Women and diabetes. I founded the DAWN Foundation (Diabetes and Women's Network) because women often come last in their own family's health priorities.
  • Access. I am a strategic partner of T1International, a patient-led organisation working for affordable insulin and supplies, and an advisory board member for diabetes organisations in Africa.
