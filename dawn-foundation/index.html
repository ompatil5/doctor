DAWN Foundation: Diabetes and Women's Network

Vision

A world in which every individual living with diabetes, irrespective of age, geography, or socioeconomic circumstance, has uninterrupted access to continuous, compassionate, and evidence-based care that addresses both the metabolic and psychosocial dimensions of the disease, and in which distance is no longer a barrier to sustained clinical engagement.

Mission

DAWN Foundation (Diabetes and Women's Network) exists to close the gap between clinical diagnosis and lived experience in diabetes care, with a particular focus on paediatric Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus (T1DM), Type 2 Diabetes care, and the psychosocial burden of chronic metabolic disease. We do this by building structured, replicable models of continuity of care, generating culturally validated screening tools for the South and Southeast Asian population, and fostering a global network of clinicians, researchers, and caregivers committed to holistic, data-driven diabetes management.

Mental health and wellness sit at the core of everything DAWN Foundation undertakes. We hold that diabetes cannot be managed as a purely metabolic condition; the psychological burden it places on people living with diabetes and caregivers is inseparable from clinical outcome, and every initiative we build is designed around this premise.

How We Work

DAWN Foundation is built around global connection, with particular emphasis on low- and middle-income countries (LMIC), where the burden of diabetes is rising fastest, and the infrastructure to address its psychosocial dimensions is often thinnest. Our approach is deliberately practice-oriented: research undertaken under the DAWN umbrella is intended not to remain confined to publication, but to be actively translated into policy and ground-level impact and change. We pursue this through:

  • Global case studies: drawing on real-world scenarios from across diverse healthcare settings to surface what works, and why, in contexts unlike our own.
  • Candid, question-driven webinars: participating in and convening dialogue that is willing to ask the difficult questions, rather than restating consensus.
  • A collaborative model: built with partner organisations over time, so that shared learning ultimately translates into improved clinical practice on the ground.

Aim

To design, validate, and scale two complementary, evidence-anchored initiatives: a sustainable continuity-of-care model for children with T1DM and their caregivers, and a cross-culturally validated instrument for diabetes-related psychological distress in partnership with international diabetes organisations, so that both may be adopted, adapted, and studied across diverse healthcare settings.

Strategic Objectives

Objective 1: A Sustainable Continuity-of-Care Model for Children with Type 1 Diabetes and Their Caregivers

Children with T1DM and their families frequently disengage from structured follow-up between hospital visits, particularly where distance, cost, or caregiver burden limit access to specialist care. This objective addresses that gap through a low-resource, replicable engagement framework built around three pillars:

  • Structured monthly progress review: scheduled visits (in-person or telehealth-enabled) to track glycaemic control, growth parameters, insulin technique, and psychosocial adjustment, using a standardised progress-tracking format that generates longitudinal, analysable data.
  • Caregiver-inclusive awareness content: a structured library of short awareness and education videos addressing insulin management, hypoglycaemia recognition, nutrition, school integration, and caregiver mental health, designed for repeated use between visits.
  • Distance-agnostic engagement: a sustainable module allowing children and caregivers in remote or underserved areas to remain continuously engaged with the care team despite geographic separation, reducing loss-to-follow-up and enabling earlier identification of clinical or psychosocial deterioration.

The model is intended to generate prospective outcome data (glycaemic, anthropometric, and distress-scale trends) suitable for joint publication and for adaptation by partner organisations operating in comparable resource settings.

Objective 2: Cross-Cultural Refinement and Validation of the Modified Diabetes Distress Scale (mDDS)

Diabetes distress is a well-established, independent predictor of glycaemic outcomes, yet the majority of validated distress instruments were developed and normed on Western cohorts, whose sociocultural stressors, family structures, and health-system contexts differ substantially from those of South and Southeast Asian populations. In response, the Modified Diabetes Distress Scale (mDDS) was developed and has already undergone initial validation, with the supporting paper published and indexed on PubMed.

  • Current stage: refinement of the validated South and Southeast Asian instrument into a monograph-length work, expanding on item development, psychometric performance, and population-specific distress domains.
  • Next stage: cross-continental adaptation and validation, in collaboration with partner organisations, to establish the mDDS as a culturally responsive alternative or complement to existing distress scales across diverse populations.

This objective is offered as a specific, concrete avenue for collaborative multi-country research; data collection, translation and back-translation, and comparative psychometric analysis are all natural points of partnership.

An Invitation to Collaborate

DAWN Foundation is seeking partnership with international diabetes organisations that share this commitment to closing gaps in continuity of care and to culturally responsive, evidence-based tools for psychosocial assessment in diabetes. We welcome the opportunity to discuss how our ongoing work in paediatric T1DM engagement, T2DM, chronic disease conditions, and diabetes-distress research might align with and contribute to your organisation's priorities.
